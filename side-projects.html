Project 01

Daikibo Industrials: A Forensic Data Investigation

Two open questions from a fictional four-factory manufacturer (Meiyo/Tokyo, Seiko/Osaka, Berlin, and Shenzhen), run as a Deloitte Data Analytics Job Simulation, Forensic Technology track.

Task A · Tableau

Which Machines Keep Breaking?

Daikibo's tech team had a month of IoT telemetry sitting in one nested JSON file: May 2021, four factories, nine machine types each, a status ping every ten minutes, 160,704 records deep. Every "unhealthy" ping stood in for ten minutes of possible downtime. The brief: find where to send the maintenance crew first.

  1. 1Imported the nested JSON into Tableau, expanding every schema level so location.factory and data.status resolved as usable fields, not buried objects.
  2. 2Built a calculated field, Unhealthy, worth 10 for every unhealthy ping and 0 otherwise, to quantify downtime in minutes.
  3. 3Charted Down Time per Factory and Down Time per Device Type as two linked sheets.
  4. 4Combined both into one dashboard, so clicking a factory's bar filters the device-type chart to that factory alone.

Task B · Excel

Where Did Pay Stop Being Fair?

After internal complaints about gender pay inequality, Daikibo's Forensic Tech team had already scored every Factory × Job Role pair on an Equality Score from −100 to +100, where 0 is parity: 37 pairs across 4 factories and up to 11 roles each, from Machine Operator up to C-Level. The scores existed; nobody had classified them yet.

ClassScore rangeReading
Fair−10 to 10Within noise of parity
Unfair±11 to ±20Consistent, correctable gap
Highly Discriminativebeyond ±20Structural, not statistical noise

Each row's class ran on a single nested condition, checking the score's distance from parity in order of severity:

=IFS(ABS([@Score])<=10, "Fair", ABS([@Score])<=20, "Unfair", TRUE, "Highly Discriminative")

The formula was the easy part. The actual skill was upstream of it: deciding where the category boundaries should sit, and turning a raw −100 to +100 number that only an analyst can interpret into a plain-language label a non-technical stakeholder can act on without asking what the score means.

Verified against Deloitte's model answer key: zero discrepancies across all 37 rows.

Project 02

Legends of Aether: Preparing a Stakeholder Review

A Forage Product Management Job Simulation: as a PM Intern on a fictional mobile game team, working alongside Maya (Lead PM), Jordan (Product Data Analyst), and Priya (Player Marketing Manager) to recommend the right KPIs and plan a stakeholder presentation.

Task A · KPI Recommendation

Matching Business Questions to the Right Metric

Given 11 standard mobile game KPI definitions, matched each of four business scenarios to the metric that actually answers it, with justification for why, not just which. Matched the official model answer with zero discrepancies.

  • New player retention→Day 7 Retention Rate
  • Size of the daily active community→Daily Active Users
  • Previously active players going inactive→Churn Rate
  • Effectiveness of an in-game purchase campaign→Conversion Rate
Task B · Stakeholder Presentation Plan

Choosing What Belongs in Front of Leadership

Jordan supplied six candidate findings. Each was run through a four-question content priority check: does it support the objective, is it relevant to stakeholders, does it reveal something meaningful, and does it support a decision. Four survived; two didn't.

  • KeptDay 7 retention declined among new players after the latest update, the core risk and headline finding.
  • KeptSurvey responses suggest onboarding is confusing for new players, the likely root cause behind the decline.
  • KeptSession length rose among players who completed onboarding, the upside case for fixing it.
  • KeptPlayers acquired through the recent marketing campaign retain worse than existing players, the segment-level view of the same risk.
  • CutStrong event participation among already-engaged players, which didn't move the decision at hand.
  • CutMost popular character color is blue, not relevant to the objective.

The four kept findings were sequenced into a 7-step project plan, each step assigned an owner, expected output, dependency, and review point:

Benchmarked against Forage's own model answer: same core logic, matched on all four review criteria (logical sequence, clear ownership, useful outputs, and review points). The model splits validation into two separate steps by owner rather than one combined step; the task specification confirmed exact step structure didn't need to match, so this wasn't corrected.
